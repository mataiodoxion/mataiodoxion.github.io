#retrospective

Proof of Experience#

Didn't take selfies, but did receive business card from one of the panelists.

Before the Event#

Q: What did you expect before attending?#

I was expecting a sort of conversational manner to the panel, and not really large reactions from the panelists we were presenting to. We presented to a couple people working in the transportation sector as well as a man in cybersecurity, but there was always the risk of presenting a very anti-Flock message to a pro-Flock person. Generally, I didn't have any other expectations except just going with the flow of the event.

Q: What were you curious about?#

I was pretty curious about how the event would pan out and the perspectives of our attendees; mainly, I was concerned with the manner of our presentation which tended to be pretty frank with its opposition to Flock. Of course, such straightforward presentation of the situation with very clear opinions is a pretty double-edged sword, and so most worries stemmed from the reactions this would garner.

Given the panelists' professions in transportation and cybersecurity, the statements we put out could fall both ways: maybe they support the use of ALPRs, or maybe they're succinctly against them. Needless to say, the result of such backgrounds was really something to look out for.

What I saw#

Q: List booths, speakers, projects, classes, or presentations you visited.#

We saw two other presentations running at the same time in the CS classroom: Safe Passage Heals and Unified e-Sports, which I frankly found to be uninteresting (given my focus on our own presentation). I didn't have much time to stick around, so I just jumped out of there.

Outside of the classroom, I could see some other CTE presentations, but I didn't linger for long because I had somewhere to be.

Q: What was the most interesting thing you saw?#

Maybe not a visual thing, but one of our panelists mentioned her involvement with the LAPD in developing First Drone Responder (FDR) platforms. She also made a point in that they weren't mass surveillance, but did actually increase the safety on policing pretty greatly, although I'm not too sure how much our own DeFlock message resonated with our audience.

Nonetheless, she also made mention of San Diego council member Monica Montgomery Steppe who heavily opposes the use of ALPRs within San Diego -- and, IIRC, she was only 1 of 3 council members who voted against the renewal of the contract. Funnily enough, I heard the name as Monica Steph, and blanked on the spot, but it came back to me later (sorry!).

What Inspired Me#

Q: What inspired you the most?#

Q: Why did this connect to your personal interests?#

I'll connect the two questions here. I was initially inspired by an article published by the EFF a few years back detailing the use of ALPRs as methods of mass surveillance, but it never really clicked for me until recently when I started noticing the black apparatuses of the cameras on my daily commute to school. (My AP World teacher actually attended our panel and posed this question about my inspiration and interest!).

As for personal interests, I've always been interested in the expansion of technofascism and associated mass surveillance systems, so the proliferation of Flock Safety was certainly a prime example to follow. The aforementioned panelist was kind enough to refer us to councilmember Steppe, and I've been inspired by the gesture to try expanding our reach and indicate our support for her cause as well. The entire point of our presentation was to spread awareness, and the questions posed by our listening panelists further corroborated that vitality of awareness in trying to get this idea passed.

Q: How does this connect to your CS classes, projects, or future goals?#

The insight of the industry professionals we made contact with at the expo really inadvertently widened by view of awareness in trying to make social change. As much as I'd like to admit otherwise, I sort of botched the point I was trying to make about the need for awareness and common support (as in a critical mass of people) to truly create change -- maybe analogizing it to the resistance against data centers in Utah. Nonetheless, the guiding questions by our panelists had led me to believe that the best next step is to establish rapport with the city itself (and especially councilmember Steppe).
